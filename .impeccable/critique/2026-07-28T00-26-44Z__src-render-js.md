## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The *idea* is unusually specific and could not be lifted onto another product without lying. Conserved matter as both visual system and engineering claim. A caret that is a literal read/write head, consuming one page's glyphs to emit the next. The hourglass as mark, idle state, and buffer parking lot at once. Mono-speaks-as-machine / Archivo-speaks-as-person. A boot log whose text is a true statement about the code. `↻ rebuilt … from the GitHub API` generated, not typed. This is authored-for-this-product at a level most portfolios never reach, and CONTEXT.md's vocabulary discipline is visible in the source.

The *executed page* is far less specific, and the gap is measurable. The 90th-percentile pixel inside the `<h1>` "SHUBHAM" box sits at **1.04:1** against `#0a0c0e`; the brightest pixel in the whole word reaches 5.85:1. The hourglass, which carries no information, reaches **19.59:1 with a pure white pixel**. The visitor never watches matter resolve into a letterform — they watch dim stipple sit next to a bright abstract mark. Strip the concept you can only learn by reading source, and what remains is: near-black field, one cyan accent, particle text, monospace corner HUD, scramble-on-hover nav, fake terminal boot. That stack ships on a hundred developer portfolios. The conservation — the one thing that is genuinely yours — is invisible, because you cannot watch grains move *between two named things* when neither name is readable.

**Blunt call: the concept is specific; the render is generic dark-portfolio-with-particles, because the conservation never resolves into type.**

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json src/render.js dist/index.html` → **exit 0, clean.** One advisory `em-dash-overuse` (14 em-dashes, `dist/index.html`) — a **false positive**: only 6 are in body prose against a threshold of 8; 4 are `<head>` metadata (two of them verbatim duplicates) and 4 are machine-chrome separators that DESIGN.md's §Typography explicitly sanctions.

## Overall Impression

You have built something genuinely rare and then hidden it behind its own effect. The dual-reading architecture, the vocabulary discipline, the honesty of the copy, and the design system's internal consistency are all top-decile work — the deterministic scan proves the system holds under mechanical inspection. But the site fails at the one thing it exists to do: a visitor cannot read your name. Every named element built from particles renders at roughly 1:1 contrast, while the decorative hourglass renders at pure white. The hierarchy is inverted by construction, in the shader.

The single biggest opportunity is subtraction, not addition. 6,000 grains are spread across 17 matter elements — about 350 per heading, drawn at 3.6–6.6px points across a 120px cap height. Spend them on fewer things. A site where only the name is matter and everything else is crisp type would be a *stronger* claim, not a weaker one.

## What's Working

**1. The dual-reading architecture is correct in a way that is genuinely rare.** One HTML, zero duplicated content, a `<head>` probe (`render.js:34-37`) that decides before first paint so neither mode flashes. B verified both degraded paths independently: under `prefers-reduced-motion` and with JS disabled entirely, all four pages go `visibility: visible`, the document scrolls normally at 2691px, the boot terminal never blocks, in-page anchors still work, and 900+ chars of prose are reachable. Most sites attempting this ship a stub and call it a fallback. This ships a second designed document — and bluntly, it is the better-designed of the two. It works because the calm reading was treated as a layout problem, not a degradation problem: `.matter` overrides are *removed* below 820px rather than patched.

**2. The boot terminal holds a system rule under real pressure.** The three window dots are `--dim / --dimmer / --line` (`style.css:622-635`), not red/amber/green. That is exactly where a designer normally caves, because borrowed chrome is *asking* for saturation. Holding it means the visitor's first cyan is the site's cyan — the single-hue reveal isn't spent on window furniture. The code comment explains why, so the next editor can't undo it by accident. It's a small decision that pays for the whole color story.

**3. Copy and implementation are coupled, and the copy doesn't overclaim.** `6,000 units · nothing lost` is true of `N = 6000` and a buffer never reallocated. `every glyph on screen is conserved matter, never created twice` is true of the transition. The rebuild date is generated from `builtAt`. Voice is first-person, plain, no adjective stacking. For an audience of developers, the fact that nothing on the page is a claim they could disprove by opening devtools is worth more than any effect.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Matter text never resolves — the hierarchy is inverted in the shader

**What.** Measured luminance, 90th-percentile pixel inside each glyph box at 1920×1080, against `#0a0c0e`:

| Element | p90 | p99 | brightest pixel |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<h1>` SHUBHAM (120px) | **1.04:1** | 1.51:1 | 5.85:1 |
| "Experience" heading (54px) | 1.03:1 | 1.86:1 | 5.71:1 |
| "something real." (84px) | 1.04:1 | 1.55:1 | 5.38:1 |
| `j4khmola@gmail.com` (28px) | 1.03:1 | 2.44:1 | 14.27:1 |
| repo name `career-ops` (27px) | 1.03:1 | 2.34:1 | 16.50:1 |
| *epoch `<h3>` — plain DOM text (16.5px)* | **15.14:1** | 16.60:1 | 16.60:1 |
| *hourglass sigil — decoration* | 1.05:1 | **15.96:1** | **19.59:1 (255,255,255)** |

Two mechanisms, both in the shader path. **Density**: ~4,800 grains across up to 17 `.mt` elements is ~300 per glyph run, drawn at `baseS * 0.6 * GRAIN(2.3)` ≈ 3.6–6.6px points across a 120px cap height — far below what reads as a letterform. **Mode**: settled glyph grains get `dT = 1` (`matter.js:892`), routing them to the soft-dot atlas cell with `glint × (1.0 - vD) = 0`; idle hourglass grains get `dT = 0.2` (`:912`), routing them to the *terminal-glyph* cells **with** chromatic fringing and up to `0.55` glint. **The decoration is drawn in the bright mode and the content in the dim mode.**

Independently, B measured the floor breach in CSS: `.pname` computes **below 26px for every width 821–1181px** (25.96 at 1180, 22.528 at 1024, 22 below 1000 — `style.css:364-368`), and `.email` computes below 26px for **821–866px** (`:402-407`). And `style.css:362` comments *"Floor at 22px"* where DESIGN.md §The 26px Floor says 26. The system contradicts itself, and the 22–27px band is measurably the least legible content on the site.

**Why it matters.** This is the product. PRODUCT.md Principle 1: "If a visitor remembers one thing, it should be the thing they watched happen." Nothing legible happens. It also breaks the One Hue Rule in fact if not in code — the brightest region of the site is achromatic white.

**Fix**, in order of payoff:
1. **Cut the matter-built set.** `.pname` (5 elements at 27px), `.year` (4 at 42px) and `.tagline` (26px) consume ~60% of the budget at sizes the grid cannot resolve. Demote all three to DOM text; let matter carry the four page headings, the name, and the contact statement. Same buffer, ~3× density per glyph.
2. **Raise `N`** (`matter.js:24`) to 14–18k. The buffer is sized once and never reallocated, so this costs memory, not per-frame allocation — it does not violate the performance constraint. Re-check the adaptive DPR downscale at `:757-763`.
3. **Swap the draw modes.** Give `st === 1` grains the glyph atlas + glint the idle grains currently get; give idle grains the dot. Clamp accumulated output so the brightest achievable pixel is `#67e8f9`, not white.

**Where.** `static/matter.js:24, :52-69, :156, :161, :266, :891-892, :912`. `static/style.css:220-226, :284-290, :364-368`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder` (on the matter render specifically), then `/impeccable typeset`.

### [P1] The home page breaks at 821–1279px and again at ≥2820px — and the rule guarding it names the wrong element

**What.** `.home-top` wraps, dropping the 180×260 sigil onto its own row and collapsing the composition. Both agents flagged it; I re-measured it myself with the real Archivo/JetBrains Mono loaded:

```
821 WRAP   900 WRAP  1024 WRAP  1100 WRAP  1152 WRAP  1200 WRAP  1250 WRAP
1279 ok    1280 ok   1366 ok    1440 ok    1600 ok    1920 ok    2560 ok   2800 ok
2820 WRAP  3000 WRAP 3440 WRAP  3840 WRAP
```

**The composition only holds from 1279px to 2819px.**

DESIGN.md:200 states the budget as `min(1180, 84vw) − 180 − 5vw` against a name measuring `~7.16 × font-size`, and names 1920 and 1024 as "the two tightest points." **Below ~1500px the binding element is not the name.** `.tagline` is fixed `26px` mono with `0.14em` tracking and `white-space: nowrap`; it measures **808.1px at every single viewport width** — it never responds to anything. It shares `.home-head` with the name, so it sets that column's base width. The name only takes over above 1600px, where it caps at 859.3px.

Two things follow. First, the rule's own arithmetic *predicts* the 1024 failure — `860 − 180 − 51 = 629 < 859` — so the check it tells you to run was written down but never actually run. Second, it doesn't contemplate the ≥2820px case at all, where the fixed 1180px cap stops growing while the `5vw` gap keeps going.

Note for the record: B independently **refuted** the older "name wraps to two lines between 820 and 1200" bug — `.name` returns exactly 1 client rect at all 30 widths tested. That bug is fixed. This is a different failure: the flex *row* wraps, not the name.

Separately, at 821–877px the tagline is **clipped, not wrapped** — 808px of nowrap text plus an 8vw gutter exceeds the viewport, and `.page` is `overflow:hidden`, so "…DEEP LEARNING" is silently cut. At 1920 the tagline's right edge sits at 1178.1 inside a 1180px column: **1.9px of slack.**

**Why it matters.** 1024×768, 1152×864, iPad landscape, a window snapped to half of a 2560 monitor (1280 — 25px of margin), half of a 2400 (1200 — broken), and **opening DevTools on a 1440 laptop** all land in or next to the broken band. That last one is the single most likely action of the primary audience.

**Fix.** Make the tagline responsive (`clamp(16px, 1.6vw, 26px)` — which drops it below the matter floor, another reason to demote it to DOM text per P0), and take the sigil out of the flex row entirely: `position: absolute` against `.page-in` so it can never force a wrap. Then re-derive the budget against `max(tagline, name)` and add the ≥2820px case. DESIGN.md:200 must be rewritten, not just the CSS.

**Where.** `static/style.css:206-211, :220-226, :228-242, :484-487, :715-718`. DESIGN.md:200.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The transition can only be watched once per page, and `sessionStorage` makes that permanent

**What.** `const full = first || !this.visited[page]` (`matter.js:589`). First visit to each page gets 1.7–1.9s of full choreography — caret sweep, consume, emit. Every subsequent visit gets a 0.62s direct flight with none of it. `this.visited` is persisted to `sessionStorage` (`:743-745`) and restored on load (`:204-206`), so **a reload does not restore the choreography** — only a new tab does.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md's primary user "will replay the page transition two or three times before reading anything," and the stated success metric is "someone posting a screen recording of the transition." The code makes that impossible. The thing they came to replay is spent after one viewing per page and cannot be recovered in the same session. This inverts the product's own success metric, in four lines of code.

**Fix.** Either drop the `visited` gate entirely and always run the full choreography, or keep the fast path only for *rapid* re-navigation (a short time-based cooldown, not a permanent per-page flag) so a deliberate return still gets the show. If the fast path exists for perceived responsiveness, that is what the 500ms input cooldown at `:1022` is already doing.

**Where.** `static/matter.js:204-206, :589, :743-745`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable animate`

### [P1] The email is an invisible tab stop whose focus ring is composited away

**What.** `<a class="mt email">` (`render.js:178`) inherits `.matter .mt { opacity: 0 }` (`style.css:565-569`). Verified with real dispatched `Tab` keys:

```
tab stop 5 on contact → { text: "j4khmola@gmail.com", opacity: "0", focusVisible: true, outline: "solid 2px" }
```

`:focus-visible` matches and the outline computes correctly — but `opacity: 0` composites the entire element **including its outline** to nothing. A keyboard user's focus lands on an element that is invisible, unreadable, and whose focus state cannot be perceived. It is the site's primary CTA.

Compounding it: **no `aria-live` region exists anywhere**, and the only status indicator (`#hud-page`) is `aria-hidden="true"` (`render.js:123`). Pressing `3` moves a sighted user to Projects and tells a screen-reader user nothing. B confirmed zero `[role]` attributes in the document and no skip link — the first tab stop is the brand mark.

Also: the `<h1>`'s accessible name is **`"SHUBHAMJAKHMOLA"`** and the contact `<h2>`'s is **`"Let's buildsomething real."`** — the two-span split has no whitespace between spans. One-character fix at `render.js:135` and `:177`.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md: "role, email, and the resume must be reachable within one interaction from any page." Reachable is not perceivable. DESIGN.md:182 specifies this element as "clickable at every width" — it is clickable and unreadable, the worst combination for the one piece of information someone might want to copy rather than click.

**Fix.** Exempt `.email` from matter entirely and render it as DOM text in Signal Cyan (13.52:1). It is 28px; it will never resolve from particles, and per P0 it should not be spending grains. If it must stay matter, move the `opacity: 0` onto an inner `<span>` so the outline lands on a visible parent. Add `<p aria-live="polite">` fed by `setHud()`. Add the missing space between name spans.

**Where.** `src/render.js:123-127, :135, :177, :178`. `static/style.css:402-408, :565-569`. `static/matter.js:1057-1068`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Projects dissociates the name from the action, and has no empty state

**What.** Three problems on the page that carries the actual evidence.

*Association.* The `52px 1fr auto` grid pins `source →` to the right rail. At 1920 the name ends at x=610, the description at x=1068, and `source →` starts at **x=1486** — 418px from its own description. Below 820px it drops to its own grid row, 41px from its description and 20px from the divider separating it from the next row: closer to the boundary than to its content. Gestalt proximity fails in both directions.

*Empty state.* With 0 Featured Projects the page renders an illegible "Projects" heading in a void plus an orphaned `all repos on github →`, and nothing else. Content comes from a live API on an unattended nightly cron, and PRODUCT.md itself warns that "a rebuild that fails silently leaves stale content up with no one watching." *Degenerate content.* A one-character repo name renders as a few pixels of stipple — a functionally empty row. Repos with missing summaries all emit the identical string `See the repository for details.`, which reads as a bug rather than a fallback.

**Fix.** Move `source →` under the description inside `.pbody` and make the whole row the link target — the right rail buys nothing at any width. Vary the missing-summary fallback using the repo's primary language and star count, which the API already returns.

**Where.** `src/render.js:65-75, :169-171`. `static/style.css:335-347, :726-732`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard` (empty/edge states), then `/impeccable layout`

## Minor Observations

- **The Closed Ramp Rule holds only at the clamp endpoints.** Every value at 1920 is a documented rung and the union across widths is exactly the 22 steps — but between each clamp's min and max the computed size is continuous. 115.2, 102.4, 81.92, 61.44, 45.056, 26.4, 25.96, 24.63 and ~20 others are all real computed sizes and none is a rung. The rule is true as written and not true of what renders.
- **The boot terminal runs 2.11s, not the ~1.25s DESIGN.md documents.** First legible page copy lands at ~2.96s from navigation. The doc and the code disagree by 0.86s.
- The calm reading **never marks an active nav item at any width** — `matter.js:20` early-returns without `.matter`, so `navActive()` never runs, and there's no CSS-only fallback. Four identical links across a 2,691px document.
- Two stacked bottom rows with different left margins: `#pg-contact footer` uses `left: var(--gutter)` (8vw = 115px at 1440) while `.hud` uses `3.2vw` (46px). A 70px misalignment on adjacent baselines.
- Google Fonts is a hard third-party dependency with no local `@font-face`, and `sampleAll()` is gated behind `document.fonts.load()` **with no timeout** (`matter.js:436-452`). A slow — not failed — font CDN stalls the entire matter site, not just the type. A uBlock "block remote fonts" rule changes every metric the Sigil Budget Rule depends on.
- The parked caret lands ~180px past the last visible glyph on home because `parkCaret` uses `lt.r`, the layout box of a `nowrap; fit-content` span, not the ink extent. It reads as a floating triangle in dead space, not a cursor at the end of a line (`matter.js:768-776`).
- `.matter .brand { margin-left: 40px }` is a magic number clearing a 28px sigil; it doesn't track the sigil's size.

- The `github / linkedin / resume.pdf` row is byte-identical on home and contact with no differentiation of role.
- Pitch and about share size, weight, colour and gap — twelve lines of undifferentiated grey at the calm site's most important content moment.

## Questions to Consider

2. The transition is the metric. The code makes it shorter and less choreographed the second time you see it, and persists that. Who is that optimisation for?
3. The calm reading is measurably the better-designed of the two sites. What changes if you design the matter reading *from* it — adding particles to a legible page — instead of hiding content to make room for particles?
4. 6,000 grains across 17 matter elements is ~350 per heading. What is the fewest headings you could afford at full density? Is that number 1? Would a site where only the name is matter, and everything else is crisp type, be a *stronger* claim?
5. The hourglass is the brightest object on every page, carries no information, and is the element that forces the home row to wrap at eleven of the twenty-four widths measured. What is it for?
